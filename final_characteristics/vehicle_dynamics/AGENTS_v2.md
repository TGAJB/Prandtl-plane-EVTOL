The task is to create a script that finds the controllability limit for various flight phases for a Prandtl Plane. The controllability limit corresponds to the most forward CG longitudinal location (either from the aircraft nose or the LEMAC of the front wing) for which the full moment generating capabilities of the elevators are used to trim the aircraft.

The solution procedure to find controllability limit for a given aircraft configuration and flight phase is underconstrained. This means that the problem is not uniquely solvable and the solution procedure contains iterative processes. The solution procedure for a Prandtl Plane therefore differs than that for a conventional aircraft.

The aircraft is subjected to two conditions in each flight phase for each aircraft configuration:
    1. The total generated lift equals the aircraft weight;
    2. The total pitch moment about the aircraft CG is equal to zero.

Assumptions:
    - C_L_alpha does not vary with control surface deflections;
    - The influences of C_L_alpha and C_L_delta_e are linear and superimposable;
    - Flapelevons can be used as elevators;
    - The flapelevons of the front and the aft wing are identical;
    - Control surface deflections do not affect the location of the neutral point.

Global instructions / recommendations:
    - Make a callable function that takes as input an instance of the class 'Aircaft', the MTOW (in kilograms), the location of the neutral point for the given aircraft instance, the local air density, and the aircraft airspeed;
    - The function must output the controllability limit in meters as measured from the aircraft nose and the controllability limit normalised by the MAC of the front wing with the LEMAC as datum;
    - The attributes of the taken instance must be used to find the values of the relevant parameters.
    - The CG-envelope that is to be investigated to find the controllability limit spans from the aircraft nose to the neutral point;
    - First place the CG at the neutral point, and then progress to the aircraft nose;
    - The flapelevons deflection angle envelope is defined as the envelope from the minimum deflection angle to the maximum deflection angle;
    - The script should be called 'det_control_limit.py'

Solution manual:
    1. Use the lift curve slope of the entire aircraft to find the lift coefficient of the total aircraft and the effective angle of attack of the front wing based on the provided local conditions;
    2. Use the effetive AoA of the front wing to find the downwash angle that the aft wing experiences and find the effective AoA of the aft wing;
    3. Find the lift coefficients of the front and the aft wing using the provided local conditions, MTOW, and the downwash. Use the lift curve slopes that are stored in the aircraft instance!;
    4. Find the sum of the separate lift forces generated by the front and the aft wing (taking into account pressure ratios between the front and aft wing etc.) and print it as a percentage of the MTOW;
    5. Iterate over the CG-envelope:
        i. Select a CG-location;
        ii. Iterate over each possible combination of elevator deflections:
            a. Select a combination of deflection angles for the front and aft wing flapelevons;
            b. Compute the new values for the front and aft wing lift coefficients;
            c. Compute the total lift that is generated by the wings;
            d. Compute the total pitch moment about the CG;
        iii. Plot and save the results per CG location (same type of HTML plots that were created in the scripts that investigated the static long. stability):
            a. Make a 3D-plot with the deflection angles of the front and aft wings (in degrees) on the x- and y-axis, and the corresponding values for the total generated lift on the z-axis;
            b. Make a 3D-plot with the deflection angles of the front and aft wings (in degrees) on the x- and y-axis, and the corresponding values for the total generated pitch moment on the z-axis;
        iv. Interpolate the surfaces s.t. functions for L_tot and M_tot are obtained as functions of the deflection angles;
        v. Find the combination(s) of deflection angles for which the total pitching moment is zero;
        vi. Determine whether the total generated lift for the found combination(s) of deflection angles in v. is (at least very near) equal to the MTOW in a small neighbourhood around the combination(s) of deflection angles. If this condition is true, then the current CG-location falls within the controllability limit;
    6. Display the most aft CG-location for which the controllability condition is not satisfied.
